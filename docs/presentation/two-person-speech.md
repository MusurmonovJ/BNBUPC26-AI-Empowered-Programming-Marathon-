# Lethe — two-person speech

Oct 14, 2026 · @Turar

Goes with the deck [lethe-contest-5min-bilingual.pdf](lethe-contest-5min-bilingual.pdf).

## Who says what

A talks to the room and asks the questions; B drives the laptop and says short, calm lines that can be read from a card. A speaks about 3 minutes, B about 1 minute, and the live garden fills the rest.

| Slide | Time | Speaker | Why this person |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · think of one memory | 0:00–0:25 | A | Opening question, eye contact |
| 2 · two memories from one life | 0:25–1:05 | A | Storytelling, the "imagine" lines |
| 3 · the rule | 1:05–1:25 | B | Three short factual lines |
| 4 · one light | 1:25–1:40 | A | Asks for hands |
| 5 · live garden | 1:40–3:10 | B drives, A narrates lightly | B's hands on the game, few words |
| 6 · a seed remains | 3:10–3:35 | B | Quiet slide, quiet voice suits it |
| 7 · why we called it Lethe | 3:35–4:05 | B | Calm, reflective, can be read |
| 8 · make your own | 4:05–4:30 | A | Takes the floor back, warm and direct |
| 9 · ending | 4:30–5:00 | A, then both | Return to their memory, vote, thank you |

**A (more outgoing):** you own the questions and the pauses. Your job is to make the room feel included, not to perform.

**B (quieter):** you never have to improvise. Every line is short, and the English is already on the slide behind you, so a blank moment is fixed by reading the slide. B also holds the clicker for the whole talk.

## The script

The whole talk runs 5:00, with spoken words finished by about 4:55. Words in [brackets] are actions, never said aloud. Replace [A] and [B] with your names.

### 1 · Think of one memory — 0:00–0:25 — A

**A:** Hi everyone. Before we show you anything, think of one memory you'd like to visit again. Maybe a voice. A song. A place. You don't have to tell anyone — just keep it.

[five seconds of quiet; smile, look around the room]

**A:** Got one? Hold on to it. We're [A] and [B]. For "Your Life as a World," we built Lethe — where the pieces of a life become a garden.

[B clicks]

### 2 · Two memories from one life — 0:25–1:05 — A

**A:** [gesture left] Imagine someone who always knew what to say to you. In this made-up life, that's Grandma Mei. Once, she said —

[slow down] "You don't have to be brave all the time."

[two-second pause] She and her city, Hangzhou, hold up seven memories.

[gesture right] Now imagine a place that was only yours. Somewhere you went when you needed the sky. Here, it's the library rooftop. Nothing else in this life holds it up. And as it fades, it forgets the details first — the name, the time.

**A:** [B], how does that work?

[B clicks]

### 3 · The rule — 1:05–1:25 — B

**B:** Every flower is one memory.

[B clicks; the second line appears]

**B:** When two memories share a person or a place, a thread of light joins them. They hold each other up. That's why Grandma Mei is held — and the rooftop is alone.

[B looks to A, clicks]

### 4 · One light — 1:25–1:40 — A

**A:** Here's the hard part. There's one light left. Think of the memory you picked at the start. Was it someone — [point left] like Grandma Mei — or somewhere [point right] like the rooftop? Raise a hand for the one you'd keep.

[raise your own hand for a second as an example, then stay quiet about five seconds]

**A:** Okay — [Grandma Mei / the rooftop] it is. [if no hands: "Let's go with Grandma Mei."] [B], let's see what it costs.

[B switches to the game]

### 5 · Live garden — 1:40–3:10 — B drives, A narrates

The full moment-by-moment plan is in the next section. Spoken lines only:

**A:** Watch where the light goes. And listen.

**B:** [after tending] Now we plant one new memory. [reads it] "Osmanthus tea with Grandma Mei on her balcony in Hangzhou."

**A:** See the new threads?

**B:** And now, a season passes.

[both silent through the fading, then four more seconds]

**A:** Nothing here is scripted. The same rules decide every outcome.

[B returns to the slides]

### 6 · A seed remains — 3:10–3:35 — B

**B:** Some flowers kept their colour. Some became outlines — or seeds.

[pause]

**B:** A seed still keeps its words. You can always go back to it.

[B clicks]

### 7 · Why we called it Lethe — 3:35–4:05 — B

**B:** That's why we called it Lethe. In Greek myths, Lethe is the river of forgetting.

**B:** Forgetting is part of every life. We wanted it to feel gentle, not like losing something. A meaningful life can have quiet spaces too.

[B looks to A, clicks]

### 8 · Make your own — 4:05–4:30 — A

**A:** Grandma Mei is made up. But your garden doesn't have to be. You can plant your own memories — words, songs, places, photos. It lives only in your browser. No account, nothing stored on a server. If you turn on the optional AI tagging, it sends just the memory's text and names, to suggest connections.

[B clicks]

### 9 · Ending — 4:30–5:00 — A, then both

**A:** Remember the memory from the beginning? What would you grow around it?

[two-second pause; B clicks]

**A:** You can't keep every moment in bloom.

[B clicks]

**A:** What you tend gives the garden its shape.

[B clicks]

**A:** Try Lethe at lethe-nine.vercel.app, on a computer. And if it stays with you, we'd really appreciate your vote.

**A and B together:** Thank you.

[stand still and hold the last slide five seconds]

## Live demo choreography

B's hands stay on the laptop for all 90 seconds and A speaks only in the gaps. Silence during the fading is part of the show, so nobody fills it.

| Time in demo | B does | A says |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15 s | Opens the memory the room chose, tends it once | "Watch where the light goes. And listen." |
| 15–35 s | Picks the tea fragment, shows its preview, plants it; reads the line aloud | "See the new threads?" once the bloom finishes |
| 35–65 s | Says "And now, a season passes," then lets it run | Nothing. Four seconds of quiet after the fade ends |
| 65–90 s | Pans to the result and one surviving thread, then back to the slides | "Nothing here is scripted. The same rules decide every outcome." |

What to say depends on what the room chose:

| Room chose | What happens with the tea planted | A can add |
|---|---|---|
| Grandma Mei | Her words keep their colour; the rooftop becomes a seed | "The rooftop had nothing holding it." |
| The rooftop | It survives as a faint outline (a ghost); Mei's words lose their colour | "She was held by others, but her own words faded." |

Never call a surviving rooftop "lost." If the room kept the rooftop, slide 6 still works: B introduces it as an example of a faded memory, not as their rooftop.

Rehearse the actual controls on the venue laptop beforehand. Have a second tab ready on the three-minute film in case the live garden fails, and say so honestly if you switch.

## Rehearsal tips and fallbacks

Run the full talk three times with a phone timer; most teams run 20–30 seconds long at first, and the fix is cutting words, not talking faster.

**For A**

- After a question, count to five in your head before moving on. Silence feels longer to you than to the room.
- Ask once. Don't repeat the hands question or count votes.
- Hand over by name ("[B], how does that work?"). It tells B when to start without a rehearsed signal.

**For B**

- Put your lines on one small card or in the speaker notes. Reading is fine.
- Look at the screen while you speak, not the audience. It looks natural because you're showing them something.
- Speak slower than feels normal. Your slides are the quiet ones, and slow fits them.
- If you blank, read the English line on the slide behind you. It says almost exactly what your line says.

### If something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| No hands go up | A: "Let's go with Grandma Mei," and move on |
| The room splits evenly | A picks whichever side looks slightly bigger; no counting |
| The live garden freezes or won't load | B switches to the film tab; A says "This recording follows a fixed choice" |
| Running long at 3:30 | A skips the AI-tagging sentence on slide 8 |
| Running long at 4:15 | Skip "What would you grow around it?" and go straight to the two closing lines |

The slides already carry the Chinese, so neither of you needs to translate. Speak simple English and let the screen do the rest.
