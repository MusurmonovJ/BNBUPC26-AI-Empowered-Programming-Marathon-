# Lethe contest speeches

The central idea is that memories become a world through their relationships and the attention we give them. Use the 90 second script over the film, or the three minute speech with the six slide presentation. Keep the fading scene quiet long enough to feel its change.

The example life belongs to fictional characters. Grandma Mei is part of that example, not a claim about the presenter's own family. The scripts describe the approved episode route; rehearse against Claude's finished build before recording. Keep the garden's original memory quotations intact.

## The 90 second voiceover

This version follows the episode film. Speak at roughly 120 to 130 words per minute during spoken passages. Leave room for the animations and silence. Begin timing after the opening gesture and scene readiness.

### 0 to 8 seconds

> A sentence. A song. A place. What would a life look like as a world?

### 8 to 17 seconds

> Grandma Mei once said, “You don't have to be brave all the time.” Her memories connect.

### 17 to 23 seconds

> Here is the library rooftop. A place remembered on its own.

### 23 to 39 seconds

> I plant another moment: tea with Grandma Mei in Hangzhou. Watch it grow, then find the memories it belongs beside.

Let the bloom and first thread finish. Do not keep speaking through the whole interval.

### 39 to 49 seconds

> One light remains. I choose her words. The light reaches their neighbors. Caring for one memory can help hold others.

### 49 to 53 seconds

> Now, three weeks pass in this garden.

### 53 to 66 seconds

> Some colors soften. A familiar place fades.

Finish this line early. Stay silent through the rooftop's final dissolve and the four second hold. Do not click return.

### 66 to 80 seconds

> A seed remains. We can return to it. For now, I let this garden show both what stayed and what faded.

### 80 to 90 seconds

> You cannot keep every moment in bloom. What you tend gives the garden its shape. This is Lethe.

Leave the closing image on screen. Put the site link in the video's final frame or description. Keep the request to vote outside the philosophical ending.

## Questions after the demo

### Are memories really erased

> No. A lost flower leaves a seed, and its original words remain. Returning costs light. The fading is how the garden expresses changing attention, not permanent destruction of someone's memories.

### Why does a connected memory last longer

> Shared people and places create relationships. Those relationships strengthen retention, and tending sends some care to neighbors. The benefit has a limit, so adding endless threads does not make the garden immortal.

### Is this how human memory actually works

> It is an artistic model inspired by forgetting curves. I use it to make attention and connection visible. I do not present it as a medical model of memory.

### Where do personal memories live

> A personal garden stays in this browser. Planting has an offline tagger. If an optional tagging provider is enabled, memory text and known entity names can go to that provider to improve suggestions.

### What did you build technically

> A procedural 3D garden in React Three Fiber, with a separate TypeScript simulation. Vitality comes from time, tending history, and relationships. Local storage keeps personal gardens, and the contest episode uses the same engine with an explicitly paused demo clock.

### What would you improve next

> I would improve the first personal planting, especially connection previews and correction of people and places. Then I would expand the authored life into longer chapters after the short experience is clear and reliable.

## Delivery notes

- Pronounce Lethe as LEE thee. Say Grandma Mei naturally; do not pause to explain the name during the film.
- Speak in your own voice. Do not claim the fictional memories happened to you.
- Look at the audience during the opening and closing. Let them watch the important animations.
- Keep the philosophical interpretation as an invitation. People do not need to agree that particular memories should be forgotten.
- When presenting your work, describe it as an artistic memory world. Avoid claims about therapy, neuroscience accuracy, guaranteed survival, or guaranteed contest results.
- Use the exact voting destination supplied by the organizers. The site link is not automatically the voting link.
- If the deployed build still uses the original reference demo, rehearse its time advance and script separately; do not describe the new one-light episode as already available there.
