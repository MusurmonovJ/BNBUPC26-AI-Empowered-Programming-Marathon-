# Lethe three minute contest speech

Choose the 3 min route in the presentation toolbar. This route uses eight of the twelve slides. Keep the live game segment to 60 seconds, with one choice, one planting, and the seasonal transition. The separate 90 second film voiceover remains in speeches.md.

The example life is fictional. Describe the outcome actually shown in the game. The slide interactions record a kind and a room choice only in memory; they do not run the garden simulation or tally audience votes.

## 0:00–0:15 A life becomes a garden

> For “Your Life as a World,” I built Lethe: a garden where memories become flowers.

Stand still for the opening. Look at the audience. Let the picture settle before speaking.

## 0:15–0:30 A memory of your own

> Think of one memory. Keep it to yourself. Is it a sentence, a song, a place, or a photograph?

Leave five seconds for thought. Choose a kind if someone volunteers one. Never ask for a personal story.

## 0:30–0:50 Grandma Mei’s words

> In this fictional life, Grandma Mei said, “You don’t have to be brave all the time.” Her kitchen and music share the same person, so threads join them.

Read the quote warmly. Pause. Move your gaze from the flower to the audience rather than reading every label.

## 0:50–1:10 One light and your choice

> One light remains. Grandma Mei’s words, or the library rooftop? Raise a hand. Let’s take your choice into the garden.

Ask once, then stop talking. Select the room’s choice. Accept either answer without praising one as correct. No tally or personal data is recorded.

## 1:10–2:10 The living garden

> Watch where the care travels. I’ll plant a fragment, then let a season pass.

Open the verified game. In the short route allow 60 seconds for real actions and silence. In the showcase allow 90 seconds. If using the rehearsed film, explain that it follows its own fixed choice. Never pretend the film responds to the room.

## 2:10–2:25 The quiet space

> A seed remains. Its words are still there. We can return later. For now, leave this space quiet.

Hold four seconds of silence. If the room kept the rooftop alive, discuss another actual lost seed or loss in general. Do not claim their rooftop disappeared.

## 2:25–2:45 What holds this life

> The garden reveals the people and places holding this life together. A different choice can leave a different shape.

Reveal the names only after the picture settles. Keep your voice quieter than at the opening.

## 2:45–3:00 What will you carry

> You cannot keep every moment in bloom. What you tend gives the garden its shape. Try Lethe. I would appreciate your vote.

End on the audience’s own imagined memory. Say the voting invitation once. Hold the final view.

## Delivery and audience engagement

- Begin with the room, then the project. Give people time to think before asking for a response.
- Ask for raised hands once. Accept either memory without judging it. If the room stays quiet, say “I will choose Grandma Mei for this first visit,” then proceed.
- Move deliberately. Put the cursor down while the flower grows or fades. The scene needs time to be seen.
- Speak plainly during the rules, warmly during the quotation, and more quietly during the ending. Let silence carry the loss scene.
- When using the recording, say “This recorded visit follows my choice of Grandma Mei.” Do not imply it responds to the room.
- Rehearse with a timer. Shorten explanations if you overrun; preserve the bloom and four-second quiet hold.
- Put the verified live URL and real voting destination into the links dialog before the audience arrives. Open speaker view on your private display, then fullscreen the deck on the projected display.
- End with an invitation once. Give the audience a clear route to trying and voting, without promising contest results.
